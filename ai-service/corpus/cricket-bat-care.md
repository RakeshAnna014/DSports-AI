# Cricket Bat Care and Maintenance

> SAMPLE DSports guide, written for the demo knowledge base from general cricket knowledge. It is not manufacturer
> documentation. Always follow the maker's own instructions where they differ.

## Knocking-in a new bat

A new willow bat is soft. Knocking-in compresses the fibres so the face does not crack or dent. Use a proper bat mallet (or the
rounded edge of an old ball in a sock) and tap the face and edges with gradually increasing force. Plan for several hours spread
over a few days, then play with an old ball in the nets before a new match ball. Never knock the bat with a hard object that has
sharp edges.

## Oiling

Apply a very thin coat of raw linseed oil to the face, edges and toe, and let it soak in flat for a day. One or two light coats
are enough; too much oil makes the bat heavy and soft. Do not oil the splice or the handle.

## Protecting the blade

Fit a toe guard to stop moisture entering the bottom of the bat and apply edge tape if the bat does not have it. A face
protector (anti-scuff sheet) protects the face from scuffs and moisture, but check the bat is fully knocked in first.

## Storage

Keep the bat in a dry place at a stable temperature, in a cover and standing or lying flat. Do not leave it in a hot car, near a
heater or in a damp garage, which can crack or warp the wood. Never store it wet.

## After play

Wipe off dirt with a dry or lightly damp cloth, dry the bat, and check the toe and edges for cracks. Small surface cracks can
often be repaired by a bat repairer; a deep crack through the blade usually cannot.

## What to avoid

Do not use a leather-ball bat with a hard tennis ball with tape, do not play with a wet ball, and do not hit the ball with the
very edge or toe on purpose. Do not soak the bat in oil or water.

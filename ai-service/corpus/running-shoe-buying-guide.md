# Running Shoe Buying Guide

> SAMPLE DSports guide, written for the demo knowledge base from general footwear knowledge. It is not manufacturer
> documentation. DSports does not currently list a running shoe in this sample catalogue; this guide explains how to choose
> one and how the same ideas apply to other sports footwear.

## Fit comes first

Try shoes on in the afternoon, when feet are slightly larger, and wear the socks you will run in. There should be about a
thumb's width of space in front of the longest toe, a snug heel that does not slip, and no pressure across the widest part of
the foot. A shoe that feels slightly wrong in the shop will feel worse after 10 kilometres.

## Cushioning and feel

More cushioning softens landings and suits long, easy runs and heavier runners. Less cushioning feels firmer and faster and
suits short, quick sessions. Neither is better in general; choose the feel that stays comfortable at the end of your usual run.

## Stability and your foot

If your ankles roll inwards a lot when you run, a stability shoe with a firmer inner side can help. If you have a neutral
stride, a neutral shoe is usually enough. If you are unsure, a gait analysis at a running shop is more reliable than guessing
from the arch of the foot.

## Surface

Road shoes have smoother, flatter soles for pavement. Trail shoes have deeper lugs for grip on mud and loose ground and are
heavier and stiffer. Indoor court shoes (badminton, volleyball, basketball) have gum rubber soles for grip on wooden floors
and should not be used for road running, and spiked cricket shoes are for grass only.

## Weight and breathability

Lighter shoes feel quicker but give less protection. Mesh uppers keep feet cooler; a more structured upper holds the foot
better. For hot climates choose a breathable upper.

## How long do shoes last

Most running shoes keep their cushioning for roughly 500 to 800 kilometres. Replace them sooner if you notice new aches,
flattened cushioning or worn-through soles.

## Looking after them

Let wet shoes dry at room temperature with the insoles out, away from direct heat. Do not machine-wash them unless the maker
says it is safe.

# Cricket Bat Buying Guide

> SAMPLE DSports guide, written for the demo knowledge base from general cricket knowledge. It is not manufacturer
> documentation and does not describe any specific DSports product unless it names one.

## Start with the player, not the price

Pick a bat for how the player hits, not for the name on the sticker. The three things that matter most are the player's
skill level, how heavy the bat is for them, and the ball they play with (leather or tennis).

## Skill level

**Beginner**: choose a forgiving bat with a large sweet spot, a medium or light pick-up, and a durable blade. English willow
is not necessary to learn the game; a good lower-grade blade lasts longer for someone who is still finding their timing.
**Intermediate**: players who bat regularly in club cricket benefit from a lighter pick-up and a better grade of willow,
which gives more power from the same swing. **Advanced**: high-grade English willow with a high, powerful profile is built
for players who consistently hit the middle of the bat.

## Weight and pick-up

Most adult bats weigh between about 1.1 kg and 1.3 kg. A lighter bat (around 1.1 kg or less) is easier to swing, helps you
play late and is easier on the wrists. A heavier bat (around 1.25 kg or more) carries more power for strong hitters but
tires younger or newer players quickly. Weight alone does not decide pick-up: two bats of the same weight can feel different
because of where the wood sits. If you are unsure, choose the lighter of two bats that fit your height.

## Size

Bats come in sizes by height. As a rough guide, a player under 135 cm uses a smaller junior size, a player around 165 to
175 cm uses a short handle adult size, and taller players use a full-size or long handle. Hold the bat in your batting
stance: the toe should rest on the ground without you stooping or reaching.

## Willow

English willow has a softer, more forgiving fibre and is the usual choice for leather-ball cricket. Kashmir willow is harder
and heavier, lasts longer and costs less, which makes it popular for tennis-ball and practice cricket. The grain count on the
face is a rough guide to quality, but more grains do not always mean a better bat.

## Leather ball or tennis ball

A bat made for leather-ball cricket is not meant for hard tennis balls with tape, which can crack the blade. If you play both,
keep a separate bat for each.

## Before the first game

A new bat needs knocking-in before it is used against a hard ball. See the Cricket Bat Care guide for how to do it, and
fit a toe guard and edge tape if the bat does not have them.
